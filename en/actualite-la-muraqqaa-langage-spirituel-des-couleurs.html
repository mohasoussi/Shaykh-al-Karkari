Shaykh Mohamed Faouzi al-Karkari on the Muraqqaʿa: the spiritual language of colours and of the divine Light

On 19 November 2026, the University of Chicago, in collaboration with The Zawiya Foundation, organised an exceptional meeting bringing together Shaykh Mohamed Faouzi al-Karkari and Professor Janet, curator at the Art Institute of Chicago, whose work focuses in particular on the cultural and symbolic dimension of textiles.

The meeting began with a presentation by Professor Janet on the traditional garments of Africa and Asia, through their protective, cultural and spiritual dimensions. Building on this presentation, Shaykh al-Karkari offered a profound reflection on the Muraqqaʿa (المرقعة), the traditional Sufi cloak made of pieces of fabric, and on its meaning within the Karkariya path.

He traced its origin back to the Prophet Adam — peace be upon him — who, in Paradise, gathered leaves to cover himself. He also drew a symbolic link with the shirt of the Prophet Joseph — peace be upon him — through which his father regained his sight.

Drawing on the Qur’anic verse: “Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth” (24:35), the Shaykh explained that the Muraqqaʿa symbolises the glass that surrounds the lamp, while its different colours represent the Most Beautiful Names of Allah.

He distinguished the Darbāla, a garment made from discarded fabrics, intended to cultivate humility, patience and detachment from worldly appearances, from the Muraqqaʿa, made up of twelve colours corresponding to the twelve letters of لا إله إلا الله (“There is no god but God”).

One of the most striking reflections of his talk was his description of the whole universe as a Muraqqaʿa:

“God created the universe as a Muraqqaʿa, assembled from colours.”

Through this perspective, colours, geometry, architecture and artistic forms become a spiritual language, revealing meanings that go beyond their outward appearance.

The Shaykh stressed that the disciples do not wear the Muraqqaʿa simply as a sign of belonging to the Karkariya path, but as a means of disciplining the ego and expressing the spiritual realities of the heart.

The Muraqqaʿa is much more than a garment. It is a language of the remembrance of God, of humility, of divine Beauty and of the Light carried in the heart of the human being.
