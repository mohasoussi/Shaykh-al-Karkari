The Study of the Divine Names: Shaykh Mohamed Faouzi al-Karkari at the University of Chicago

On November 19, 2026, Shaykh Mohamed Faouzi al-Karkari joined Dean Maurice Charles at the University of Chicago for an enriching interfaith conversation titled “What Is in a Divine Name?” The evening brought together reflections from Islamic and Christian spiritual traditions, exploring the Divine Names, the nature of faith, the reality of God’s light, and the transformative power of divine love.

The Shaykh began by reflecting on how God defines Himself through Lā ilāha illā Allāh (There is no god but God), explaining that the Divine Names are not merely titles, but manifestations of God’s attributes through which creation comes to know its Creator. He emphasized that the universe itself is a reflection of these Names, inviting humanity to contemplate creation as a path toward knowing God.

Drawing upon the Qur’anic Verse of Light (24:35), the Shaykh explained that faith is a light God casts into the heart, a light that grows through righteous actions, remembrance, and sincere devotion. Through the example of Prophet Abraham’s contemplation of the star, moon, and sun, he explored the spiritual journey from outward observation toward inward recognition of divine light.

He also reflected on the Virgin Mary, presenting her devotion and solitude in the sanctuary as an extraordinary example of complete love and surrender to God. Her story illustrated how spiritual intimacy is cultivated through remembrance, sincerity, and freedom from worldly distractions.

At the heart of his teachings was a profound reminder: God loved us before we loved Him, and He is nearer to us than we are to ourselves. The Shaykh invited everyone to examine how much time they dedicate to worldly ambitions compared to the time they devote to their Creator. He encouraged setting aside even a small portion of each day for solitude, prayer, repentance, and intimate conversation with God.

The discussion continued with reflections on sincerity, spiritual companionship, and the importance of surrounding ourselves with people who remind us of God. The Shaykh also shared aspects of his personal spiritual journey, describing how hardship, solitude, and years spent contemplating nature taught him the language of divine love beyond books and formal instruction.

Throughout the evening, Dean Maurice Charles offered reflections from Christian mystical theology, particularly the writings of Pseudo-Dionysius, drawing meaningful connections between the two traditions.

The gathering concluded with an open exchange about Jesus, the Virgin Mary, and the theological differences between Christianity and Islam. While affirming the Islamic understanding of Jesus as a prophet and Mary as a model of devotion, the Shaykh emphasized the importance of approaching one another with humility and respect, seeking what unites us rather than what divides us: the light, mercy, and love of God.

The evening at the University of Chicago ended with a heartfelt prayer for illuminated hearts, mutual understanding, compassion, and unity among people of different faiths.

A beautiful gathering that reminded us that beyond intellectual discussion and theological reflection lies a deeply personal invitation: to turn inward, seek the light within our hearts, and rediscover our relationship with the One who created us.
