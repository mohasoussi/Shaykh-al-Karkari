Shaykh Mohamed Faouzi al-Karkari invited to the University of Chicago for a discussion on near-death experiences

On 19 November 2026, we had the honour of welcoming Shaykh Mohamed Faouzi al-Karkari to the University of Chicago, where he was invited to speak to a group of students as part of a course devoted to near-death experiences (NDEs).

During this particularly moving meeting, the Shaykh shared the intimate account of three experiences in which he found himself facing the nearness of death, thus offering a singular perspective on mortality, consciousness and the spiritual realities that go beyond our physical existence.

Death as a passage between two worlds

The Shaykh began his talk by presenting the Sufi understanding of death as a passage between the mulk, the material world, and the malakut, the spiritual world.

Between these two dimensions lies the barzakh, the intermediate world that both separates and connects the two realities.

Drawing on his own experience, the Shaykh then recounted three deeply formative episodes of his life: a suicide attempt in his youth, a vision of the grave experienced during a spiritual practice of remembrance, and then a life-threatening poisoning.

These experiences deeply nourished his reflection on divine judgement, the meaning of repentance, and the transformative power of spiritual companionship as well as of the khalwa, the spiritual retreat.

From trial to divine mercy

Through this testimony, the Shaykh explained how a path that had begun in suffering, isolation and the nearness of death ultimately opened the way to a profound experience of divine mercy, light and closeness to God.

At the heart of his testimony lies a particularly powerful saying:

“I chose death, and my Lord chose life for me, so that you would know that it is my Lord who chooses, and not you, nor I.”

This saying sums up an essential dimension of his experience: a human being may go through moments when he believes there is no way out, but divine mercy can manifest itself where man sees no possibility at all.

A reflection on divine mercy and love

Beyond the exceptional nature of these experiences, the Shaykh insisted on a message that concerns every human being: God’s mercy and love are never absent, even in the darkest moments of existence.

No human being is ever definitively cut off from divine compassion. Suffering, hardship and even the nearness of death can become, by God’s grace, the beginning of a deep inner transformation.

This meeting thus made it possible to establish a particularly rich dialogue between the academic approach to near-death experiences and the lived experience of a living spiritual tradition.

Where university research meets the Sufi tradition

We express our deep gratitude to Dr Yousef Casewit and to the University of Chicago for this invitation and for creating a space where academic research and lived spiritual experience could meet in an open and constructive dialogue.

The Zawiya Foundation is honoured to be able to contribute to this kind of reflection and dialogue, by making known the wisdom of the living Sufi tradition and by putting it in perspective with contemporary questions about consciousness, death and human experience.

This meeting at the University of Chicago thus bears witness to the possibility of a fruitful dialogue between spirituality, inner experience and academic research, around questions that touch one of the deepest enquiries of human existence: what happens when we find ourselves at the border between life and death?
